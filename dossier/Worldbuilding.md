# Master Worldbuilding Questionnaire

### 1. Geography, Environment & Nature
1. What is the terrain like? Are there mountains, deserts, forests, floating islands, tropical areas, or grasslands/plains?
2. How are the continents laid out, and how connected are they?
3. How are the countries laid out, and how do differences in gravity, atmosphere, or celestial bodies (multiple suns or moons) affect the world?
4. How much land is in each of the equatorial, temperate, and polar zones?
5. What is the climate like, and is it consistent or changeable?
6. How do the seasons work (how many are there, how long are they, and what types exist—e.g., spring/summer/autumn/winter, dry/wet, or decade-long winters)?
7. What is the weather like, and how does it affect people's way of life and daily routines?
8. What natural resources are available in different regions, which are scarce, and who controls them?
9. What natural disasters and threats exist (e.g., magical storms, deadly fog, volcanic activity), and how do people prepare for them?
10. Are there any unusual plants and animals (birds, small/large animals, reptiles, insects, sea creatures), and are they unique to specific regions?
11. Are there regions considered sacred, cursed, or uninhabitable due to supernatural or natural forces?
12. Have human or non-human activities affected the landscape and climate over time?
13. How do extreme environments (e.g., underwater cities, volcano dwellings) impact daily life?
14. What are the primary modes of long-distance travel and navigation (roads, portals, flying creatures, maps, constellations, magical compasses)?
15. What are the local ecosystems like, including food chains, keystone species, and how native flora and fauna have adapted to any magical or extreme environmental pressures?

### 2. History and Lore
16. What major events, historical eras, or catastrophic shifts shaped the world and its current geopolitics?
17. Who are the historical heroes, villains, and legendary figures whose exploits are turned into myths or prophecies?
18. Has magic always existed, or was it discovered, invented, or gifted by gods?
19. How do different cultures interpret the past, and how far back do recorded histories go (written vs. oral tradition)?
20. What are the ancient ruins, lost civilizations, sunken lands, or undiscovered territories that still hold deep secrets or powerful artifacts?
21. What is the oldest known piece of writing or art?
22. How did the first civilizations develop—were they guided by gods, magic, or natural evolution?
23. Are there hidden histories, such as events that have been erased, altered, or suppressed by those in power?
24. Have there been revolutions, uprisings, or major social shifts that changed the world?
25. Are there long-standing feuds, bloodlines, or ancient rivalries that still impact the present?

### 3. Population, Society and Culture
26. What sentient non-human species exist, did they evolve locally or come from elsewhere, and what is the relationship between different species?
27. What is the total population of the planet, countries, or major cities?
28. Are the people diverse or uniform, and what is the social hierarchy (caste systems, nobility, class system, levels of citizenship)?
29. How big is the gap between rich and poor, is there slavery, and is social mobility easy or hard?
30. Where and when did civilization begin, and do most people live in rural or urban areas?
31. What are the standard family units, how are children raised, and do people marry for love, politics, or other reasons?
32. How are genders treated, and what roles do family structures play?
33. What are the cultural values (e.g., honoring strength vs. compassion, wealth vs. generosity) and what constitutes honor or shame?
34. What are the social rules, including gestures of respect, rude gestures, curses, social faux pas, and conversational taboos?
35. What does beauty mean in this society, and what symbols, colors, or insignias represent power, luck, or status?
36. How do different cultures handle hospitality, gift-giving, and treatment of outsiders, nomads, or foreigners?
37. What are the major traditions, festivals, rituals (birth, marriage, death), and special celebratory foods or clothing?
38. What types of entertainment exist (theatre, magical storytellers, combat arenas, games, festivals)?
39. What are the primary forms of art, do people value art, and who can afford it?
40. What are the cities, houses, and general architectural styles like—is architecture indulgent or functional?
41. What do people wear in daily life, and what is in fashion?
42. How do different communities handle public sanitation, waste management, and public health infrastructure?

### 4. Food and Daily Sustenance
43. What does a feast look like compared to a basic pauper's meal?
44. Do people mostly eat meat, fish, or vegetables, and is food plain or heavily flavored?
45. Do people eat together or separately, in small or large groups, and what utensils or table setups do they use?
46. Are there many restaurants, street food sellers, or public gathering places for dining?
47. What do people drink, and is the water generally clean enough to drink?
48. Are there any foods that are poisonous to certain people or species?

### 5. Language and Communication
49. What languages, dialects, and writing systems exist, and are most people literate or illiterate?
50. Do most people speak more than one language, and is there a common language for trade?
51. Are names significant (do they have power, are they inherited, or do they change over time)?
52. How do non-verbal communication, dialects across social classes, and regional slang evolve or create barriers between populations?

### 6. Education and Employment
53. Is there formal education, at what age do children start, and what are schools like?
54. Is education available to all or only certain groups, and are different groups educated separately?
55. What are common jobs, highly desirable jobs, and lowly jobs?
56. Are there professional guilds, institutes, or trade organizations, how do you join them, and what benefits do they provide?
57. What are the main agricultural crops, are they consumed locally or traded, and is farming local or industrially scaled?

### 7. Economy and Trade
58. What is the monetary system, what are the most common forms of currency, and are there multiple currencies or barter systems?
59. Is there widespread trade, what goods and services are traded, and what are the major trade routes?
60. What goods are considered luxuries versus necessities, and who controls them?
61. What are the economic disparities between regions, and what is the black market or smuggling like?
62. How do people store wealth (banks, treasure hoards, enchanted vaults), and how do different classes handle debt or financial hardship?
63. Are there seasons of economic boom and bust, and what causes them?

### 8. Politics, Governance and Law
64. What titles, formalities, and calendars are used?
65. What is the system of government (democracy, dictatorship, monarchy, theocracy, anarchy), is it a matriarchy or patriarchy, and who holds power?
66. How long has the system of government been in place, and how are rulers chosen, overthrown, or succeeded?
67. Do leaders have special protection (Kingsguard, secret service, spies), and how do common folk view those in power?
68. Does the government provide social assistance, centralised healthcare, public transport, or publicly run communication systems?
69. What is the system of taxation or tribute, and who enforces it?
70. What are the main political factions, ongoing wars, long-standing feuds, and external/internal threats?
71. What are the cultural views on rebellion, and are there underground resistance movements fighting the ruling class?
72. Are there secret societies, mercenary groups, assassins, or spies influencing political events?
73. What is the form of police and military, and how do they operate?
74. How are wrongdoers tried and punished, what are the penalties for treason, and are criminals common or rare?
75. What are relations with neighboring regions like (friendly, hostile, or neutral)?
76. *[Added]* How do local governments handle municipal infrastructure maintenance, emergency disaster response, and civil registration (births, deaths, citizenship records)?

### 9. Religion and Belief Systems
77. What gods, deities, or higher powers exist, and do they walk among mortals or remain distant?
78. Do different cultures have different religions, and how do religious beliefs impact laws and social norms?
79. What happens after death in this world?
80. Are there holy texts, sacred sites, pilgrimage locations, or religious artifacts holding divine power?
81. How do religious leaders gain power, and what role do temples or monasteries play in daily life?
82. What punishments exist for heretics, and are there secret cults or forbidden practices?
83. How do religious schisms, prophecies, and conflicts shape history?
84. Do people worship out of genuine faith, fear, or political obligation?
85. What supernatural beings (angels, spirits, celestial warriors, demons) serve the gods or are bound by bargains?

### 10. Magic and Technology
86. How does magic work—is it learned, inherited, bestowed, or a force of nature, and can it be depleted, stolen, or transferred?
87. What are the different forms or types of magic (elemental, divine, rune-based, blood, etc.), and what does it allow people to do?
88. What are the limitations, costs, and dangers of using magic (tiring, physical changes, madness, addiction, backfires)?
89. How is magic channeled (words, tools, physical gestures, internal focus), and what does it feel like to the wielder?
90. Who is able to and allowed to use magic, and are magic users feared, revered, or discriminated against?
91. Is magic controlled by governing bodies, guilds, or councils, and how do people prevent its misuse?
92. How advanced is technology compared to history, and do magic and technology coexist or does one dominate?
93. Can magic be industrialized (magical factories, enchantment workshops, mass production)?
94. Are there magical diseases, curses, or magical artifacts, and how are they created or controlled?
95. What ethical dilemmas arise from magic use, and what are the major historical events relating to magic?
96. How does the disposal of magical waste or hazardous byproduct materials from spell-casting and enchantment affect the surrounding environment?
